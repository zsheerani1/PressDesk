The Allotment

Every Saturday Mr Okafor arrived at plot fourteen at seven o'clock exactly, carrying a flask of tea and a radio that only received one station. He planted the same things each year: runner beans, three rows of potatoes, and a single sunflower that he measured every week with a folding ruler.

The other gardeners thought he was competing for something. He never said what.

In the August that the sunflower reached nine feet, a girl from the flats across the road leaned over the fence and asked him why he bothered measuring it.

"My wife grew one to nine feet, two inches," he said. "In 1987."

The girl came back the next Saturday, and the one after. She brought her own ruler. By September they were both standing on upturned buckets, and when the tape finally passed nine feet and three inches, Mr Okafor turned off the radio, sat down on the grass, and laughed until he had to wipe his eyes.
